## Newly resolved primary-source question

The Saudi official gazette publishes [Ministerial Decision49813,18June2021](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=17803). It adds Article73 on clearance of non-paper books previously published abroad. Its procedure calls for an electronic copy through the designated platform, an applicant with standing/evidence of the right to apply, acceptance of clearance terms, notice of subsequent changes, and compliance with content rules. It separately preserves electronic-publishing decisions. The decision also introduces immediate clearance with subsequent supervision for the referenced foreign-publication procedure.

**Narrow conclusion:** the relevant government route is not limited to shipping printed books. A proposed electronic title-clearance route can therefore be identified without purchasing a printed-book import arrangement. The text does **not** by itself establish that an Indian overseas free browser Reader is covered, that Reo Enterprise has eligible standing, that every term can be accepted, or that a separate establishment/licence must be bought. Do not turn this finding into an exemption or an issued clearance.

The [current regulator service](https://gmedia.gov.sa/en/services/clearance-of-foreign-books-and-publications) continues to expose foreign-book content distribution/publication clearance. The [official Printing and Publication Law](https://laws.boe.gov.sa/BoeLaws/Laws/LawDetails/ecaaec43-8ff9-46b8-b269-a9a700f16e66/1), particularly Articles18–22, distinguishes foreign-publication clearance/content conditions from special personal/research import situations. A consumer-facing library cannot simply adopt a personal-import exception.
