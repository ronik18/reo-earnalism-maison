Request additional information only if necessary; do not routinely demand government IDs, passwords, recovery codes or a copy of an action link. Lost-access/representative cases go to the lead for a documented safe method, not an improvised SQL export.
3. **Set the applicable deadline.** The owner confirms47-country scope, Indian company location and Indian staff; actual tester physical locations/residencies remain unconfirmed; do not invent one universal statutory clock. ## Incident procedure

1. **Start the clock on awareness.** The first handler records UTC awareness/detection and informs the lead/root immediately through established authorized internal channels. Treat unauthorized disclosure/access, credential or signing-key compromise, malicious activity, integrity loss and disruptive outages as distinct scenarios. A synthetic drill must be labelled drill and must not generate a false regulator report.
2. **Contain the affected scope.** Preserve necessary evidence before changing it; root may use already authorized session/device revocation, credential rotation or scoped service isolation as appropriate. Do not purge logs, release holds, delete suspect rows, replay payments or publish content. New paid services, architecture changes and external messages are not authorized by this document.
3. **Establish facts.** Record affected systems/data types and approximate people/records, actual geography, event timeline, current exposure, encryption/key status, provider notice/reference and actions. Preserve sanitized relevant logs with access controls/checksums; secure raw evidence privately. Ask existing provider support only through properly authorized channels; provider notification does not automatically discharge operator obligations.
4. **Assess notifications immediately.** Do not wait for complete forensics. The Indian [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf) cover relevant entities and reportable incidents; the current [official FAQ](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf),Q24/25/30, includes proprietorships and permits initial available facts followed by supplements within its reporting process. Use the six-hour reporting clock for applicable reportable incidents. The lead/root must identify the current official reporting method/form, authorize and submit the actual report when required; store acknowledgement privately. No report is sent by adding this runbook.
5. **Check other actual duties.** Where GDPR actually applies, Article33 includes a risk-based authority-notification rule with72-hour timing; Article34 separately addresses high-risk affected-person communication. Identify actual competent authority and territorial role; do not infer a representative or fill Google's declaration fields. Other connected jurisdictions and contract notice periods require their own determination. Do not promise a worldwide72-hour rule or assume every outage is a personal-data breach.
6. **Recover and verify.** Root verifies clean release/configuration, revocations/rotation and access checks. Restoring an older snapshot requires [hold/deletion reapplication before reopening](POST_RESTORE_PRIVACY_RECONCILIATION.md), matching private-media checks and unresolved-obligation review. Disaster loss of the current instruction set remains an explicit limitation. Do not reopen writes on an unqualified restore target.
7. **Close and learn.** Record cause, containment/recovery checks, notification/no-notification reasons, remaining risks, responsible follow-up and next review. Release evidence uses aggregate references only. Review whether a targeted hold is needed; age alone never releases it. A successful tabletop is process evidence, not production failover or comprehensive legal clearance.
