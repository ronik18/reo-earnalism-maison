## PK — Pakistan

**Constitution:** Article 14 protects human dignity and, subject to law, privacy of the home. It is not a comprehensive controller/overseas-transfer code. [National Assembly Constitution, Article 14](https://www.na.gov.pk/uploads/documents/1508233533_124.pdf).

**Privacy bill status:** MoITT's live legislative register labels the May 2023 Personal Data Protection Bill **Draft**. The Senate Acts register was also checked; this bounded search did not locate an enacted comprehensive personal-data statute. Therefore do not impose draft localization/critical-data/transfer provisions as current law, or claim an exhaustive absence of later legislation. [MoITT legislative register](https://www.moitt.gov.pk/Legislations); [Senate Acts register](https://senate.gov.pk/en/acts.php).

**Operative cyber nexus:** PECA 2016 section 1 covers Pakistani citizens wherever located, persons in Pakistan, and outside acts constituting an offence that affect a person/property/system/data in Pakistan. Sections 3–5, 16 and 41 concern unauthorized access/copying/interference, identity-information misuse and confidentiality. The official Code PDF's indexed text includes 2025 amendments; full PDF retrieval timed out, so this is not a completed consolidation review. The service-provider/traffic-retention scope and applicable directions still need exact current-text verification before extending routine IP-log retention on that basis. [Pakistan Code PECA](https://www.pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administrator6a061efe0ed5bd153fa8b79b8eb4cba7.pdf).

**2025 amendment:** Act II received assent and immediate commencement on 29 January 2025. Its social-media-platform definition concerns social/network services and accounts whose primary purpose is sharing user content, or content viewable/shared by other users. Private notes/progress do not alone establish that classification. Any future public-sharing functionality warrants review of enlistment/content directions; this research does not trigger a registration. [Official Gazette, sections 1–3](https://www.senate.gov.pk/uploads/documents/1738226500_897.pdf).

**Electronic transactions:** ETO 2002 section 32 expressly reaches foreign matters directly/indirectly connected with, or affecting, persons, systems or events in Pakistan. Its electronic-document recognition does not independently establish permission for all foreign disclosures. [Pakistan Code ETO, section 32](https://pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administratordbc98dd49f2df3b1d07bb986dcceb9a3.pdf).

**Consumer boundary:** Pakistan consumer legislation is not safely treated as one uniform national exemption. An official Sindh High Court judgment confirms consideration-based product/service consumer definitions under the Sindh Act; that does not decide every province or the treatment of a specific free service. No present Pakistani consumer transaction is evidenced. [Sindh High Court, R.A. S-147/2025, paragraphs 12–13](https://caselaw.shc.gov.pk/caselaw/view-file/MzExMjg1Y2Ztcy1kYzgz).

## YE — Yemen

**Constitution/cyber privacy:** The Attorney General's 14 July 2012 circular quotes Constitution Article 53's confidentiality protection for communications and judicial-order exception, and directs investigation of unauthorized interception/recording. It supports actual communications safeguards; it does not supply a general foreign-app licensing rule. [Official prosecution circular](https://www.agoye.gov.ye/page.php?lng=arabic&pprint=239).

**Enacted privacy provisions with a material scope limit:** Law 13/2012 on access to information is published by the Attorney General. Article 2 defines the relevant entity around legislative/executive/judicial authorities, central/local public/mixed bodies and entities partly/wholly publicly funded. Articles 50–55 address citizen-data legality, official-purpose necessity, written consent for third-party disclosure, purpose limitation and updating. Article 53 prohibits providing personal data to a foreign state/entity lacking comparable legal privacy safeguards. This is **not evidence of a universal Yemen-local-hosting requirement**; applicability to an Indian private proprietorship is not established. The broad wording within the privacy chapter and its relationship to Article 2 warrant a scope determination if Yemeni connections emerge. [Official Law 13/2012, Articles 2 and 50–55](https://agoyemen.net/lib_details.php?id=195).

**Consumer status:** The ministry identifies Consumer Protection Law 46/2008, dated 7 September 2008, as operative. Its primary catalogue does not resolve free foreign-library coverage or current regulations. An official 15 March 2026 ministry circular reported by state news applies Article 8 price-display duties to commercial/service establishments; this is evidence of continuing operation, not of application to Earnalism. [Ministry law record](https://www.moit.gov.ye/node/158); [official state-news report](https://www.sabanew.net/viewstory/143719).

**Remaining action:** current cyber/electronic-transaction consolidation, territorial enforcement and foreign-private-service scope remain unverified. Law 40/2006's financial/electronic-payment title must not be converted into a free-library payments/localization requirement without its exact scope. No blanket assertion of “no privacy law,” “no transfers restriction” or current comprehensive cyber clearance is justified. There is no evidenced current Yemeni user/public-body data connection requiring a new transfer permission or local registration in this receipt.

## KH — Cambodia

**Constitution:** Article 40 protects residence and correspondence confidentiality. The Constitutional Council publishes an English translation; this constitutional guarantee alone does not settle private foreign-controller transfer mechanisms. [Constitutional Council Constitution, Article 40](https://www.ccc.gov.kh/datapublic/subdata/Constitution%20of%20the%20Kingdom%20of%20Cambodia.pdf).

**Draft status:** MPTC's 5 August 2026 official announcement expressly describes validation of the Draft Personal Data Protection Law before formal legislative processing. No later promulgation/commencement instrument was verified in this search. Draft transfer/representative provisions are not operative duties on this evidence; exact status after that workshop remains a watch item. [MPTC announcement](https://new.mptc.cloud/en/2026/08/ministry-of-post-and-telecommunications-holds-validation-workshop-on-the-draft-law-on-personal-data-protection/).

**Enacted E-Commerce Law:** Royal Kram NS/RKM/1119/017 covers electronic civil/commercial activity (Article 3). Article 32 requires reasonable safeguards for electronically held personal information and prohibits unauthorized/bad-faith interference. Article 30 expressly reaches unsolicited commercial communications by businesses inside or outside Cambodia and requires an easy rejection option. Article 29 addresses sellers' identity/contact/transaction disclosures. Article 26 provides an intermediary/e-commerce licensing and online-certificate framework, with exceptions/procedures delegated to a sub-decree. These provisions do not establish that an Indian free library with only Indian staff is a Cambodian licensed intermediary; foreign-service scope and implementing exemptions remain unresolved. No general transfer-approval/localization rule was established from this text. [Government-hosted E-Commerce Law, Articles 3, 26, 29–32](https://www.khmersme.gov.kh/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Law-on-E-commerce-EN.pdf).

**Remaining action:** determine actual Cambodian civil/commercial activity and regulated-provider classification before any licensing conclusion; obtain current official implementing measures and consumer-law scope for future public supply. TRC's official register lists the E-Commerce, Consumer Protection, Criminal and Civil Codes; listing is not their complete applicability analysis. [TRC official law register](https://www.trc.gov.kh/en/laws-and-regulations/laws).
