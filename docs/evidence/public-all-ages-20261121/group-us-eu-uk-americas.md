# Public all-ages operating qualification: US, GB, BR, CA, DE, FR, DK, NL, CY

Research date: **21 November 2026**. Draft findings for root integration, based on primary government/regulator sources retrieved on that date. This is an operating-law assessment, **not book-rights clearance or a public-launch PASS**. No code, providers, scope, age limit, guardian flow, payments, book state, legacy service or physical-device acceptance was changed.

## Assessment boundary and evidence status

The requested scope is a public, free Earnalism service for all ages in the existing 47 jurisdictions. Earlier facts about Indian adult staff/testers do not qualify this audience. The reviewed [processing record](../../EARNALISM_PROCESSING_RECORD.md) describes accounts, email/Google sign-in, device/session records, reading/learning state, private notes, support, logs and overseas providers. It expressly leaves future children/special-category processing and territorial grounds unresolved. No child-specific DPIA, applicable consent-capacity decision, guardian authorization evidence, EU/UK/Brazil representative appointment or appropriate local-language notice acceptance was supplied in that record. This is a finding about reviewed evidence, not a claim that no such evidence exists anywhere.

**Anonymous reading and a child account are different processing paths.** A guest page can still disclose IP/device/session data to app/CDN/log providers. A genuinely anonymous aggregate has a different analysis from a pseudonymous session or a persistent reading profile. Account email, notes and child-linked progress cannot be treated as zero-data processing merely because access is free.

Status vocabulary: **VERIFIED RULE** means the cited primary rule/guidance was checked; **CONDITIONAL** means applicability depends on documented product/audience facts; **UNRESOLVED** means the required Earnalism assessment/control evidence is not established. No row is a jurisdiction-wide legal PASS.

## Shared EU layer: DE, FR, DK, NL, CY

[GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng), Articles **3, 5–9, 12–14, 25, 27, 28, 35–37, 44–49**: intentionally offering services to people in the Union can engage Article 3(2) even without payment; mere accessibility is insufficient. Article 8 applies when **Article 6(1)(a) consent** supports direct ISS to a child. A genuinely applicable necessity-based ground is possible, but cannot be selected as a label to evade safeguards. Article 8(3) preserves national contract law. Children merit specific protection, understandable information and privacy by design. Sensitive self-disclosures may require a separate Article 9 condition. **DPO appointment is conditional**, including large-scale regular/systematic monitoring or large-scale special-category processing, not every foreign website.

- **EU representative:** [EDPB Guidelines 3/2018, adopted territorial-scope version](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-32018-territorial-scope-gdpr-article-3-version_en), section 4, explains Article 27. An Article 3(2) controller generally needs a written mandate to a representative in a relevant member state, unless the cumulative occasional/no-large-scale-sensitive-or-offence-data/unlikely-risk exception or public-authority exception applies. An ongoing targeted account service is not shown to be “occasional.” India’s grievance officer is not an EU representative merely because an email is published. **Required next evidence:** targeting/applicability analysis and either a substantiated exception or actual appointment/mandate/contact publication.
- **Transfers:** [EDPB Guidelines 05/2021, final v2, examples 1–2](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-02/edpb_guidelines_05-2021_interplay_between_the_application_of_art3-chapter_v_of_the_gdpr_v2_en_0.pdf) distinguishes direct collection from an EU individual by a non-EU controller from a controller’s onward disclosure to its separate non-EU processor. The first is not itself a Chapter V transfer; the latter can be. **Required next evidence:** actual exporter/importer/role/access map, appropriate Article 28 terms, applicable transfer tool and effectiveness assessment for each relevant flow. “Singapore DB,” encryption or a generic DPA link alone does not establish that map or safeguards.
- **DPIA:** [EDPB small-business accountability guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/be-compliant_en) requires risk screening and explains indicators including vulnerable subjects, evaluation/scoring, monitoring and sensitive data. Two indicators are generally a useful screening signal, not an automatic safe harbor or a universal two-item legal test. **Required next evidence:** child-focused screening of progress, quizzes/learning evaluation, free-text notes, device/log processing and provider access; conduct the DPIA where likely high risk and apply relevant national mandatory lists. Do not equate ordinary private reading with a high-risk profiling system without examining actual functions.
- **Age assurance:** [EDPB Statement 1/2025](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/statements/statement-12025-age-assurance_en) supports proportionate, privacy-preserving age assurance; it does not order identity-document collection for every web page. **Required next evidence:** explain why any proposed age method is necessary/effective for the exact legal duty, minimize data, prevent repurposing and retain no unnecessary documents. No age/guardian code change is authorized by this draft.
- **Understandability:** national regulator guidance below requires child-appropriate language. There is no blanket GDPR demand to translate every UI element into every EU language. An English-only notice is nevertheless not demonstrated understandable to every intended child audience. A translated collection notice and rights/consent explanation can be a smaller change than rebuilding the app UI; actual national consumer-language rules must also be checked.

## GB: current UK-specific requirements

[Data Protection Act 2018 §9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/9) sets 13 for the Article 8 context. Do not simply copy EU transfer tools or treat a child’s contract capacity as settled by the data-consent threshold.

[DUAA 2025 §81](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/81/data.html) amends UK GDPR Article 25 with children’s higher-protection matters. **Representative/transfers:** assess UK GDPR Articles 3/27 separately from EU scope; the occasional/low-risk exception is not established for regular public accounts. [ICO territorial-scope guidance](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/4031113/ic-327905-y2y5-knowledge-hub-territorial-scope.pdf) explains the cautious occasional-processing analysis. A UK representative is a separate role from India’s contact and any EU mandate.

A separate [Ofcom scope assessment](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-rules-what-you-need-to-know) is needed only where relevant Online Safety Act service definitions are met. ## US: COPPA, not an invented general age ban

**Current rule:** [Current eCFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312) displayed Title 16 current through **19 November 2026**, retaining Part 312’s April 2025 amendment source. The [16 CFR Part 312, 2026 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2026-title16-vol1/xhtml/CFR-2026-title16-vol1-part312.html), **§§312.2–312.8, 312.10**. Under-13 child-directed/actual-knowledge collection normally needs notice and verifiable parental consent before collection, subject to enumerated exceptions. Mixed-audience classification/neutral screening is fact-specific. §312.5(c)(7) is a **persistent-identifier-only** internal-operations exception; §312.2 expressly includes functions such as authentication, personalization, security and network operations, but limits other use/contact/profile building. Do not falsely narrow this to security only or expand it to arbitrary email/notes/longitudinal profiles. §§312.8/312.10 require a written security program, risk review, service-provider assurances, minimization and a bounded public retention policy. These facts are not proved by existing adult-session tests.

[FTC FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) explain exceptions and verification methods, including appropriately qualified email-plus where no covered disclosure occurs. A generic parent checkbox, Google login or adult-owned account label is not automatically verified permission for knowingly collected child data. **Smallest assessment:** an all-ages intention alone is not conclusive child-directed classification. Map each guest/account/provider datum and use; determine general/child-directed/mixed audience status; document exception boundaries and controls. Where collection is covered and no exception applies, an owner-approved verifiable-permission design or a different, genuinely minimal-data experience is needed before that collection. This draft chooses neither.

[2025 final rule notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm) sets general amended-rule compliance for **22 April 2026**; it is not a pending obligation today. The [FTC’s February 2026 age-verification policy](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children) provides conditional enforcement discretion for narrowly used age-verification data, with minimization, deletion, notice, safeguards and accuracy conditions; it is not a blanket account-data exception or a statutory amendment. No universal federal EU-style representative appointment or GDPR DPIA is inferred from COPPA. Actual vendor assurances/security assessments still matter.

**State coverage remains unresolved:** [California AG CCPA guidance](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) describes applicability thresholds and under-16 sale/sharing authorization; the under-13 parent requirement is not a rule that every US free account requires a parent. No sale/share is established merely by hosting, and no absence is established without checking contracts/uses. [California Governor’s enacted-bill notice](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/10/13/governor-newsom-signs-bills-to-further-strengthen-californias-leadership-in-protecting-children-online/) and [Senate’s 2025 legislative summary, AB1043](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2026-01/2025-sjud-legislative-summaries-final.pdf) identify future age-signal requirements beginning **2027**; current amended text/service classification must be checked before that date. They do not establish an operative November 2026 universal browser-age-check requirement. This bounded review does not clear every US state child/privacy statute or injunction.

## BR: LGPD plus operative ECA Digital

[LGPD, Law 13.709/2018](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm), **Articles 3, 7, 9, 11, 14, 18, 33, 38, 41**: Brazilian-targeted collection can apply regardless of establishment. Child data needs best interests, simple accessible explanation and appropriate safeguards; consent, where used for children, has specific parental requirements and reasonable verification. Article 38 permits ANPD to require an impact report. Article 41’s responsible contact and any small-agent exception require their own assessment. [ANPD Enunciado 1/2023 explanation](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/anpd-divulga-enunciado-sobre-o-tratamento-de-dados-pessoais-de-criancas-e-adolescentes) confirms appropriate Article 7/11 grounds can apply with best interests prevailing. Do not state either “all child processing must use consent” or “legitimate interests automatically solves it.”

[Law 15.211/2025, consolidated ECA Digital](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/l15211.htm), **Articles 1, 8–13, 17–19, 37, 39–40, 41-A**: operative **17 March 2026**, applies to services directed at or likely accessed by minors, with risk management/age-appropriate protection duties. Article 40 provides for a Brazil legal representative; Article 39 modulates specified duties, including 40, according to functionality, influence, users and size. Do not infer an exemption from being small or foreign. Article 9’s strict access verification concerns prohibited/inappropriate under-18 content; it is not the sole rule for all literary reading. **Unresolved:** record actual service classification and proportionate duties rather than claiming every website must collect ID.

[Decree 12.880/2026](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2026/decreto/d12880.htm), **Articles 22, 24–25**: the editorial/licensed/literary-content age-assurance accommodation is **conditional**, including suitable child profiles and supervision/access restriction mechanisms. It is not an unconditional “books are exempt” route. Age measures must be proportionate/minimal and not repurposed. **Owner decision required:** determine whether to implement a qualifying accommodation/other lawful age-appropriate model; do not introduce a declined guardian feature under this research task.

[ANPD implementation timetable](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/eca-digital/) shows August–November 2026 adaptation/monitoring and planned enforcement from January 2027, expressly not excluding other enforcement at any time. These are not a statutory repeal or general safe harbor. Final guidance/regulatory changes after the cited page’s June update need a final pre-public-opening check.

**Transfers/local language:** [Resolution CD/ANPD 19/2024](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/institucional/atos-normativos/regulamentacoes_anpd/resolucao-cd-anpd-no-19-de-23-de-agosto-de-2024), **Article 2; Annex I Articles 16–17**: a contractual-transfer route must use the appropriate Brazilian clauses; its transition is past, and the required transfer information is in **Portuguese**, simple and accessible. Identify actual exporter/importer and Article 33 route first. EU SCCs or a generic DPA are not automatically Brazilian clauses; direct international collection also needs a separate flow analysis. The [ANPD transfer page](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/assuntos-internacionais/transferencia-internacional-de-dados) identifies applicable mechanisms. Current agreements/Portuguese notice/representative arrangements are **not qualified** in reviewed evidence.

## CA: federal capacity plus provincial differences

[PIPEDA, §4(1), §5(3), §6.1 and Schedule 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) requires appropriate purposes and meaningful understanding of nature, purpose and consequences where commercial-activity/nexus scope applies. Zero consumer price does not alone decide commercial activity. [OPC meaningful-consent guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/appropriate-handling-of-personal-information/collecting-personal-information-and-consent/consent/gl_omc_201805/) generally expects guardian consent below 13, subject to exceptional circumstances, with maturity-sensitive processes above that. **Unresolved:** support the applicable Canadian/provincial nexus and actual youth capacity; do not replace this with a global 13-year account minimum.

[OPC’s 2026 Children’s Privacy Code](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/protection-of-children-for-businesses/childrens-privacy-code/children-code-final-report-2026/) is current interpretive guidance, not an enacted replacement for PIPEDA. It expects meaningful child-sensitive consent, high privacy/minimization, age-appropriate explanations and usable correction/deletion. It **encourages** PIAs, particularly for higher risk; do not claim every PIPEDA app has a new statutory DPIA merely because the Code discusses one.

**Québec:** [official private-sector law, P-39.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/pdf/cs/P-39.1.pdf), **§§3.3, 4.1, 8, 9.1, 14, 17**: collection directly from an under-14 requires parental/tutor consent unless clearly for the minor’s benefit; the exception is not automatic because content is educational. The law separately governs under-14 consent, project PIAs, highest privacy settings and assessment/written agreement for relevant outside-Québec processing. [CAI’s 2026 joint-report discussion, paragraphs 236–244](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/uploads/pdfs/CAI_RAP_conj_OpenAI.pdf) confirms child-consent nuance; [CAI’s business responsibility guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/responsable-protection-renseignements-personnels-entreprise) confirms relevant project PIAs. **Transfers:** [OPC cross-border-processing guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/airports-and-borders/gl_dab_090127/?wbdisable=true) requires accountability and comparable contractual/other safeguards for outsourcing, transparency and consideration of foreign law/risks. This is not EU adequacy by another name and not a blanket Canada-local-hosting mandate. Québec §17 adds its own assessment/written-agreement requirements when applicable. No general PIPEDA in-country-representative mandate is established here; an accountable privacy contact is a separate duty.

**French and consumer communications:** [OQLF’s current consumer guidance](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/droits_linguistiques/droits/langue-du-commerce-et-des-affaires.html) describes French services/products and complete French website rights for enterprises operating in Québec. [Charter of the French Language §§52.1, 55](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/pdf/cs/C-11.pdf) separately addresses software and adhesion contracts. Applicability to this Indian operator’s exact Québec activities and any exception must be determined; a global English-only choice is not evidence those duties are satisfied.

- **GB:** [CMA unfair-commercial-practices guidance, CMA207](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207) covers misleading/omitted material information and aggressive practices; zero price is not a blanket misleading-marketing exemption. - **FR:** [Law 94-665, Article 2](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000000349929) requires French for relevant offer/presentation/use/service information; actual French-market operation and statutory exceptions need a supported decision. - **BR/CA:** ECA/LGPD and PIPEDA/provincial duties above are not displaced by zero price. Additional consumer-law nexus, indirect-remuneration/business model and provincial rules remain an explicit **unresolved assessment**, not a claimed statutory exemption or invented subscription obligation. No paid/recurring activation is assumed.

## Smallest decisions/evidence to close the actual public gap

1. **Approve an actual processing design, not a fictional age exemption.** Inventory guest identifiers, account identity, notes/progress/quiz data, logs and every provider use. Determine the lawful grounds and child-capacity/permission path for each jurisdiction. All-ages reading may remain the goal without equating it with unrestricted identified child accounts.
2. **Record the child-risk/DPIA decisions and mitigation.** UK child-focused DPIA, EU high-risk screening/national-list assessment, Québec applicable PIAs, US written security/retention work, and Brazil ECA risk/age-appropriate duties are different controls. Existing adult-browser tests prove none of these legal conclusions.
3. **Resolve actual external roles and language obligations.** Determine EU/UK Article 27 applicability/exception, Brazil Articles 39–40 representative scope, actual mandates where required, and understandable/mandatory local-language notices/service information. Appointment or spending must await concrete owner authority.
4. **Complete transfer-route evidence per actual flow.** Identify parties/roles and onward recipients, apply the correct EU/UK/Brazil/Canadian mechanism, evaluate access and supplementary safeguards, and publish the required notices. A provider DPA URL alone is insufficient.
5. **Keep unknowns visible.** US state-law coverage, authoritative Cyprus text, current Québec consolidation, latest Brazil implementing guidance, national consumer transposition and service-size exemptions need final review.
