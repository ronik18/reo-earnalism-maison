## Primary-source reasoning

1. The official [Undesirable Publications Act 1967](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/UPA1967?Timeline=Off) indexed definitions encompass electronically stored publications and electronic transfer/reproduction. Consequently, browser delivery without a sale or download button is not a sufficient basis for claiming that publications controls cannot apply. This is a scope warning, not a conclusion that physical import procedures necessarily apply to this Reader.
2. [IMDA publications guidance](https://www.imda.gov.sg/regulations-and-licensing-listing/content-standards-and-classification/standards-and-classification/publications-and-audio-materials) distinguishes publisher responsibility from pre-vetting and discusses periodical permits. It does not establish a universal per-title application for every overseas electronic literary book, or a general overseas-ebook exemption.
3. [IMDA's Singapore-related political/religious guidance](https://www.imda.gov.sg/regulations-and-licensing-listing/political-or-religious-content-related-to-singapore-provided-by-individuals-groups-organisations-corporations) separates automatic internet class licensing from registration. Its category describes relevant programmes provided in/from Singapore to propagate, promote or discuss political/religious issues relating to Singapore. The page states that registration has no fee and that IMDA issues notice where required. Those facts do not themselves establish Maison's applicability or authorize an application.
4. The primary judgment [Re The Online Citizen Pte Ltd [2021] SGHC 285](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2021_SGHC_285), especially paragraphs 42–45, confirms that class-licensed status and website registration are distinct. It is a historical explanation of that regime, not a decision about Maison, foreign ebook publishers or this title.
