## Newly resolved source question

The current [Brazil ECA Digital consolidated statute, Law15.211](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/l15211.htm), Article41-A, states17March2026 commencement. Articles39–40 establish a conditional editorial/licensed-content route that can include relief from the local-representative duty. Its conditions require age-suitability/rating transparency, accessible parental controls over content, processing, interaction and commerce, and reporting channels. Being a literary library or free service does not alone establish those conditions. Article2(IX)'s editorial definition must also be checked against actual selection behavior. Article18(VI) expressly addresses Portuguese information/control options in parental tools. The Article39 route does not disapply all other duties.

The current implementing [Decree12.880/2026](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2026/decreto/d12880.htm), Article22, separately includes literary-content services in a conditional age-assurance exemption: suitable child accounts/profiles and parental supervision/access restriction are required. That is a concrete alternative to assuming every literary reader needs the same external age-verification vendor; it is not an exemption already demonstrated by a generic guardian authorization. Article24 requires proportionate, minimal, purpose-limited age assurance; where documents are collected for it, their image/copy must be discarded after extracting the necessary information. This is distinct from independently justified evidence for another purpose. Consult the applicable ANPD implementation requirements before choosing a method.

Both primary texts were retrieved today. This lane found no sufficient basis to purchase a Brazilian representative or universal child-verification vendor merely because Brazil is on the whitelist. Equally, it found no basis to mark either duty satisfied or waived for the current platform.
