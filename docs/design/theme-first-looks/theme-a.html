Chapter Seven

The Long Monsoon

The rain came the way an old friend arrives: without knocking, already certain of its welcome. Mira stood at the edge of the orchard and counted the trees her grandfather had planted, one for every year of her mother's life.

“A tree is a promise you make to someone you will never meet,” he used to say, pressing the soil down with the flat of his palm. She had not understood him then. She thought she was beginning to.

142

By noon the river had climbed two steps of the ghat. The ferryman tied his boat twice and sat under the banyan, peeling a green mango with a knife older than the village school.

Mira opened the ledger. Each column was a season; each row, a tree. Some rows ended early, in her grandfather's careful hand: lost to the storm of '91. Most ran on, year after year, the numbers growing quietly the way interest grows in a bank no one remembers opening.

She turned the page.
