## Resolved method question: an external verification vendor is not universally required by COPPA

The current [16CFR312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.5), retrieved21November and displayed current through19November2026, enumerates multiple consent methods. It includes a signed consent form returned by mail/fax/electronic scan and, for operators that do not disclose children's personal information within the rule's definition, email with an additional assurance step and revocation notice. A verified-mailbox event alone is not that complete method. The overall standard still asks whether the method is reasonably calculated to establish the child's parent. Separate consent for non-integral third-party disclosure and precollection timing must be addressed. A provider's adult-verification result cannot simply replace these requirements.

**Integration conclusion:** the absence of KWS onboarding is not evidence that every possible US consent route is legally impossible. Equally, the enumerated alternatives do not establish that Earnalism's existing generic guardian review satisfies them. Do not install a second vendor, build an ID-document flow or relabel mailbox verification as parental consent merely to clear a checklist row.

## Resolved data classification: processor presence and parent data are not automatic disqualifiers

[16CFR312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.2), retrieved the same day, excludes appropriately limited internal-operations support from its disclosure definition. The support-purpose and no-secondary-use conditions matter; the mere existence of Render, Neon, email or security providers does not by itself decide the result. The definitions also distinguish what information is collected **from a child**, including information about a parent collected from the child and combined with an identifier. Mixed-audience treatment has explicit precollection and neutral age-determination conditions; an age question added after account collection cannot satisfy those conditions retroactively.

The [FTC's COPPA FAQ, A8](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) states that information collected online from parents or other adults is outside COPPA's child-collection scope, while expecting confidentiality during consent/access handling. The FAQ itself points to the revised rule for current requirements; it is staff guidance, not the rule. Parent-only processing still has other privacy, contract, transfer and security duties.

**Integration inference:** do not automatically reject a no-disclosure/email-plus candidate solely because a prospective KWS service independently processes a parent's own submitted email. First establish whether child-derived identifiers, linked subject/account information or child metadata actually reach that recipient and for what uses. Conversely, calling the recipient a processor, hashing an email, omitting a child name or labelling an opaque subject “anonymous” does not establish eligibility. These are unresolved field-level facts, not grounds to claim an exemption.
